# Linguistic Proposals for the Indus Script

A linguistic decipherment claims that the signs record a specific spoken language and that sign values can be assigned by explicit, testable rules. No proposal below is an accepted decipherment; each is a proponent's claim.

## Dravidian approach

**Proponents:** Asko Parpola and the Finnish group (Parpola 1994). **Mechanism:** assume the underlying language was Proto-Dravidian and read signs through that assumption. **Limitation:** Burrow (1969) reviewed an earlier Finnish attempt on this assumption and judged its handling of the Dravidian material "open to considerable criticism". Salomon (1996) reviewed Parpola (1994) in the Journal of the American Oriental Society; this survey did not read that review or the book, so their arguments and the evidence offered are not summarised here. Claims about specific readings remain unverified.

## Statistical-structure approach

**Proponents:** Rao et al. (2009). **Mechanism:** compare the conditional entropy of Indus sign sequences with that of known linguistic and non-linguistic systems. Their title claims entropic evidence for linguistic structure. **Limitation:** this survey read only the record and summary line, not the full paper. Even taken at face value, detecting structure in sign sequences is not a reading, and it does not name a language. Whether such statistics can separate language from non-linguistic systems is discussed in the evaluation section, not here.

## Comparison

The Dravidian approach proposes a language and tries to assign values. The statistical approach proposes no language and no sign values. Neither is established; the first depends on an assumed language, the second on what structure can show.
