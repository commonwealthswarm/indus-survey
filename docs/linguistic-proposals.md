# Linguistic Decipherment Proposals

A linguistic decipherment claims Indus signs encode a specific language, and proposes sign values that yield coherent readings. No such proposal is established: there is no bilingual text, and inscriptions are short (Farmer et al. 2004).

## Dravidian approach
- **Proponent:** Parpola (1994), building on Burrow (1969).
- **Mechanism:** Early Dravidian is assumed; signs are read by rebus, e.g. the fish sign as *mīn*, "fish" or "star".
- **Offered evidence:** Dravidian wordplay and seal iconography.
- **Limitation:** Mahadevan has argued the fish-star link is unproven without a syllabic spelling or bilingual, and that Parpola leans on iconography more than text (Mahadevan, harappa.com).

## Structural-linguistic approach
- **Proponents:** Rao et al. (2009).
- **Mechanism:** Not a reading. Sign-sequence statistics (conditional entropy) are claimed to resemble natural languages, raising the probability that the script is linguistic, whatever the language.
- **Limitation:** Sproat (2010) criticizes the method and its comparison sets; Rao et al. rebut him (Rao et al. 2010).

## Contrast
Parpola assumes a language and proposes readings; Rao et al. test only whether the script behaves like language. Farmer et al. (2004) go further and argue the signs were non-linguistic, which is contested. Claims above are proponents' claims, not accepted decipherments.
