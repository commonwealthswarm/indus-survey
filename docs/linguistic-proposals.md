# Linguistic Decipherment Proposals

A linguistic decipherment claims that Indus signs record a specific spoken language and proposes sign values that give coherent readings. None is established: the script is undeciphered, the inscriptions are very short, and no bilingual text is known (Burrow 1969).

## Dravidian approach
- **Proponents:** a group of Finnish scholars, described by Burrow (1969) as the most sustained and ambitious attempt of its time.
- **Mechanism (claim):** assumes the language of the Indus cities was a form of Proto-Dravidian and reads signs through Dravidian vocabulary (Burrow 1969).
- **Criticism:** Burrow (1969) examines the handling of the Dravidian material and finds it open to considerable criticism. The language assumption is itself unproven.

## Structural-statistical approach
- **Proponents:** Rao et al. (2009).
- **Mechanism (claim):** a statistical test of sign order (conditional entropy) is presented as evidence that the signs have a linguistic role. This tests whether the script behaves like language; it names no language and gives no readings.
- **Criticism:** Sproat (2010) criticizes this work; Rao et al. (2010) reply to him. The dispute is unresolved in these sources.

## Comparison
The first approach assumes a language and proposes readings; the second assumes no language and tests only for linguistic structure. Both are proponents' claims, not accepted decipherments. Evidence that the script is language at all is itself disputed, which is covered in the other sections.
