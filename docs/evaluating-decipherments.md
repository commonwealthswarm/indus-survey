# Evaluating Competing Indus Proposals

This is a **proposed protocol** a reader could apply; it is not a completed experiment, and no tests were run for this survey.

## What the evidence can and cannot show

- **Short texts.** The longest Indus inscription on one surface has 17 signs and the mean is 4.6 (Farmer et al. 2004). Rao et al. (2010) put the corpus at about 1,550 lines and 7,000 sign occurrences, which limits statistical estimates; they use the NSB estimator for undersampled data.
- **Structure is not language.** Rao et al. (2010) report that Indus block entropies resemble natural languages and differ from DNA, protein and Fortran sequences, but say this does not prove the script is linguistic, and that barring full decipherment neither thesis can be proved. They also say Sproat (2010) objects that entropy similarity alone does not imply language; this survey did not read Sproat (2010).
- **Statistical practice.** Farmer et al. (2004) criticise earlier statistical claims of links to Dravidian or Indo-Aryan.

## Proposed protocol

For any proposal, linguistic or not, check each item. Each lists an outcome that would weaken it.

1. **Corpus and segmentation.** Is the corpus source stated, with sign-variant merging and sign-boundary choices fixed before analysis? *Weakened if* results change materially under other defensible choices.
2. **Fixed sign values.** Is each sign given one value by an explicit rule? *Weakened if* values change from text to text, or if flexible rebus choices let almost any reading fit.
3. **Held-out prediction.** Are values or structure fitted on part of the corpus, then tested on withheld inscriptions? *Weakened if* held-out performance is no better than shuffled or random sequences.
4. **Independent corroboration.** Do readings agree with archaeological context or with cross-text evidence not used to build them? *Weakened if* contexts or foreign-find texts contradict the reading.
5. **Controls on both sides.** Is the method run on known linguistic and known non-linguistic sequences? *Weakened if* it cannot tell them apart, or if it gives a linguistic result for non-linguistic controls.

Passing these checks would support a proposal but would not by itself demonstrate language or recover a reading.
